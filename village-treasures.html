When the work becomes the story

Village Treasures

It started as an assignment — a shop, a client, a shot list. It became a narrative film with an original score, two festival selections, and a season of recognition for the shop at its centre.

▶

Village Treasures — co-directed with Donovan Gardener

Village Treasures is an African art store in downtown Long Beach, owned by Max Vitz. The brief was to go and film the shop.

We made something else instead. Rather than describe the place, we leaned all the way into it and wrote a story that could only happen there — coming-of-age, with a turn into magical realism. Stevi, a screen-absorbed teenager dragged along on a shopping trip, finds a Kalimba on a shelf. Every time she plays it, the store fills with dancers.

That is an old storytelling tradition carried onto a larger stage, which is the thing a studio can do for a business that a promotional video cannot. David co-directed with Donovan Gardener, edited the film, and wrote and performed its score — I Call It Kalimba, now on Spotify, Apple Music and Bandcamp.

Selected, and then recognized.

Official Selection at the Pan African Film Festival and the Micheaux Film Festival.

Then a season of it — festival screenings, press, and Long Beach City Council honoring Max Vitz in chambers, with formal recognition from Mayor Rex Richardson and Congressman Robert Garcia. The film's own recognition was part of those ceremonial honors.

A film travels the way a story does. Cast, crew and every room it reaches end up carrying something of the shop with them.
