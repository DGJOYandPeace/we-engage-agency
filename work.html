Anchor case study · Nonprofit & arts

Five years with the Los Angeles Master Chorale.

From 2018 to 2022 — event films, fundraising pieces, and the Big Sing, which puts thousands of untrained voices in one room and asks them to sing together.

Arts institutions plan on a seasonal calendar, which means every renewal is a deliberate decision made months ahead by people who have already watched last year's film with their board. Being on that calendar five years running is the endorsement.

The run opened with Big Sing California, where as their editing team we delivered exactly what the Chorale wanted to showcase after their statewide event. By Big Sing 2022 our team was carrying the film the whole way — the edit, the master, and delivery. The finished project was ours to land, which is the handoff an institution only makes once it has stopped checking the work.

What we handled: the edit from the first project, then post-production end to end — assembly and edit, the finishing master, and delivery in the formats the client's own channels needed. No second vendor between the footage and the screen.

Why a reviewer should care: an event like the Big Sing happens once and is never re-shot. Handing that cut to the same partner five seasons running is a judgement about reliability under a deadline, not about taste.

Case study · Nonprofit & arts

The season a theatre came back.

Altadena Music Theatre is a non-profit that had to rebuild its season after the devastation of the Eaton fire. We made films for the company highlighting its youth musical theatre programme, supporting the funding effort to bring its full slate of programming back to the community of Altadena.

Our work spans formats. Longer pieces for the website and the funding conversation, and short films built for the feeds where a local audience actually finds a show. The centrepiece of the season was Audrey II, the carnivorous plant at the heart of Little Shop of Horrors, built by hand at eleven feet. Craft at that scale is hard to carry in a production photo, which is part of why the company wanted it filmed.

Why a reviewer should care: one client, one season, two very different formats. Work that can sit in a grant conversation, and a short built for a phone screen — made by the same team, so they say the same thing in the two places a non-profit actually needs them.

The aim was to fill the house. The company posted the cut to their own feed, and this is what came back on Instagram. People who had already been, and people saying they were going. The show has played to sold-out houses.
